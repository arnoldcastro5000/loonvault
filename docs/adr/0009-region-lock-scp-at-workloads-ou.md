# Region-lock SCP attached at a Workloads OU, not the org root

The `ca-central-1` region-lock Service Control Policy (`infra/org/`, `loonvault-region-lock` — deny all non-global actions where `aws:RequestedRegion != ca-central-1`, supporting GR05 / OSFI B-13 data residency) is attached to a dedicated **Workloads OU**, and the LoonVault member account (`<MEMBER_ACCOUNT_ID>`) is moved into that OU. The management account (`<MGMT_ACCOUNT_ID>`) stays directly under root.

The decision that forced this: **SCPs do not affect the management account** — ever, regardless of where they are attached ([AWS Organizations SCP docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/organizations/latest/userguide/orgs_manage_policies_scps.html)). Originally all LoonVault infrastructure ran *in* the management account, so any region-lock SCP applied cleanly but enforced nothing — a documented no-op (the apply was deferred for exactly this reason). Once workloads were migrated to the member account `<MEMBER_ACCOUNT_ID>` (ADR-context: new-account migration), the SCP became meaningful for the first time: any attachment point above that member account now enforces the guardrail on it.

Given that, **where** to attach was the real choice. AWS guidance is to apply policies at the **OU level**, organised by function (Security, Infrastructure, Workloads), rather than at the root or at individual accounts ([OU best practices](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/organizations/latest/userguide/orgs_manage_ous_best_practices.html)). Attaching directly to **root** was rejected: it works (the mgmt account is exempt anyway), but it is the less-recommended pattern, it triggers Checkov `CKV_AWS_363`, and it scopes the guardrail to "everything" rather than to a named workloads boundary — a weaker story for a portfolio whose whole point is demonstrating well-architected multi-account governance. A dedicated **Workloads OU** keeps the management account near-empty and exempt by design, scopes the control to the accounts that actually run workloads, and leaves room to nest Prod/SDLC OUs later. The member account is moved into the OU **out-of-band** (`aws organizations move-account`) rather than via a Terraform `aws_organizations_account` resource, because the account was created during org setup, not by this stack — importing it would make Terraform the owner of the account *lifecycle* (a `destroy` could attempt to close the account), which is not a risk worth taking to manage a one-time parent change.

On policy **content**: the `NotAction` exempt list is a deliberately strict subset of the [AWS Control Tower Region-deny baseline](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/controltower/latest/controlreference/primary-region-deny-policy.html). We exempt the global services whose endpoints are not region-scoped (IAM, STS, Organizations, Route 53, CloudFront, Shield, WAF, billing/cost, Health, etc.) plus a small set of **global-scoped S3 actions** (`ListAllMyBuckets`, `GetBucketLocation`, account-level public-access-block get/put) that resolve to `us-east-1` and would otherwise break routine operation such as `aws s3 ls`. We intentionally **do not** exempt `kms:*` or `config:*` (which the Control Tower baseline does): our CMK and any config services live only in `ca-central-1`, so keeping them inside the deny preserves a strict data-residency guarantee for data-at-rest key operations rather than relaxing it for cross-region convenience we do not need. We also omit the Control Tower `ArnNotLike` carve-out for an automation principal: the developer's deploy operations all target `ca-central-1`, and the global-S3 exemptions already cover the only non-`ca-central-1` calls that occur in normal use, so a blanket principal exemption would only widen the guardrail's blast radius for no operational benefit.

Consequences: the guardrail is enforced (the Phase-0 verification — "attempt a resource in `us-east-2` → explicit deny" — passes from inside the member account) while the management account remains unrestricted by design. New member accounts do **not** inherit the region-lock unless they are also placed under the Workloads OU — an explicit, auditable step rather than a silent default. The strict exempt list means an operator working against a non-`ca-central-1` region will be denied for nearly everything except the handful of global metadata calls listed above; this is the intended behaviour and the documented data-residency posture, not a bug to be patched by widening the list.
